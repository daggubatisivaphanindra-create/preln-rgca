# Data Provenance & Benchmark Rationale Documentation

## 1. Methodological Rationale: Why Controlled Paradigm-Matched Benchmarks Rather Than Raw Open-Domain Corpora
1. **The Nature of Laboratory Psycholinguistic Stimuli (ON99 & KO05)**: Classic psycholinguistic datasets such as [Osterhout & Nicol (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1080/016909699386310) and [Kim & Osterhout (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2004.10.002) originated as laboratory event-related potential (ERP) neuroscience experiments with human EEG participants. Their original materials were not large-scale text archives, but printed stimulus quadruplets (30–36 items) published in journal appendices. In computational NLP, [Michalon & Baggio (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/0952813X.2026.2633150) instantiated these experimental grammars as factorial matrices over a 10-noun vocabulary (producing 360 and 384 items). To evaluate our architecture against Michalon & Baggio's published models under their exact experimental setup, our study reconstructs those exact factorial paradigms while eliminating previous stimulus errors (inflection, label conflicts, frame leakage).
2. **Avoiding the Confounders of Scale and Pretraining**: Raw external NLP benchmarks such as the full [BLiMP](https://aclanthology.org/2020.tacl-1.25/) benchmark (67,000 sentence pairs across 67 disparate phenomena including wh-islands, quantifier scope, and parasitic gaps) and the full [CoLA](https://nyu-mll.github.io/CoLA/) benchmark (10,657 unstructured sentences from theoretical linguistics publications) cannot be evaluated on a compact probe architecture without deploying massive 110M–355M parameter pretrained transformer language models (BERT/RoBERTa) with open-domain subword tokenizers. Doing so fundamentally confounds the research question: any performance difference would reflect the statistical coverage of massive pretraining rather than the intrinsic capability of parallel dual-stream representational interaction. PreLN-RGCA is intentionally constructed as a lightweight (10,252 trainable parameters) architectural probe with a closed vocabulary (186 lemmas) and a 12-tag morphosyntactic inventory to isolate *representational coordination* from scale.
3. **Isolating Native Evaluation Protocols via Paradigm-Matched Suites**: Our BLiMP-inspired minimal-pair suite (200 pairs across 4 core agreement/animacy paradigms) and CoLA-inspired acceptability suite (400 items) adopt the *official task-native evaluation metrics* of those benchmarks—pairwise score discrimination s(x_good) > s(x_bad) for BLiMP, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) for CoLA—while strictly isolating the grammatical-thematic interactions targeted by our architecture.
4. **Testing Naturalistic Generalization**: To guarantee that our findings are not artifacts of rigid two-clause templates, we evaluate on the **External Naturalistic & Verbatim Published Exemplar Benchmark (240 items across 20 groups)**, which incorporates verbatim published exemplars alongside non-templated constructions with intervening prepositional modifiers (*"the cat near the gate was eating..."*), adverbs, and subordination.

## 2. Published Baseline Transcription (`results/published_baselines.csv`)
All 84 Precision, Recall, and F1 cells for [Michalon & Baggio (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/0952813X.2026.2633150) are transcribed verbatim from their Table 2 (ON99, p. 299) and Table 4 (KO05, p. 301). Their models were not reimplemented or retrained.

## 3. Surface Inflection & Zero Label Conflicts (`data/data_manifest.csv`)
All 1,584 stimulus records across ON99 (360 items), KO05 (384 items), BLiMP-inspired Minimal Pairs (200 pairs = 400 sentences), CoLA-inspired Binary Acceptability (400 items), and the External Naturalistic & Verbatim Published Exemplar Benchmark (240 items) use genuinely inflected English surface forms. Every dataset has 100% unique displayed surface sequences (`0` conflicting labels) and 100% deterministic agreement between surface tokens and morphosyntactic annotations.

## 4. Strict Lexical-Frame Partitioning (`splits/*.csv`)
Entire lexical frames (`grouping_id`) are assigned to `train`, `val`, or `test` prior to example selection (`ON99`: 10/2/3 frames = 240/48/72 items; `KO05`: 11/2/3 frames = 264/48/72 items; `BLiMP`: 18/2/5 frames = 144/16/40 pairs; `CoLA`: 17/3/5 frames = 272/48/80 items; `External_Naturalistic`: 14/2/4 groups = 168/24/48 items). Exactly `0` lexical frames appear in more than one split.
